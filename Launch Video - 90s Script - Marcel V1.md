## The script

> **[BEAT 1 — SETUP · ~18 sec · 47 words]**
>
> Hey, what's up everyone? Marcel here. Ready for a hot take?
>
> There's this thing going around that to survive as a marketer now, you have to become a *Marketing Engineer*. Build agents. Manage your "AI stack." Get technical or get replaced.
>
> I think that's wrong.

> **[BEAT 2 — REFUSAL · ~14 sec · 41 words]**
>
> We get to work with some of the fastest-growing companies in the world — Lovable, Webflow, Ramp, Brex, Sentinel One — and not one of them needs another tool in their stack. They need to ship more, better content, faster. They need leverage, not a side career in engineering.

> **[BEAT 3 — DIFFERENT BET · ~26 sec · 72 words]**
>
> That's why we built GrowthOS.
>
> It's a closed-loop system. The frontier AI work — context, research, planning, writing, optimization — all happens *inside* the platform, end to end. You bring the strategy and the taste. We do the engineering for you.
>
> You're not orchestrating agents. You're not babysitting a stack. You're shipping content that compounds. That's the whole point.

> **[BEAT 4 — NEW STAKES · ~26 sec · 68 words]**
>
> And here's why this matters right now.
>
> Unless you've been living under a rock, you know your buyers aren't starting on Google anymore. They're asking AI. Agents are reading the web on their behalf, building shortlists before anyone ever lands on your site. Your website *is* your visibility surface now. The brands that scale trustworthy content win this. The ones that don't, disappear.

> **[CTA · ~6 sec · 11 words]**
>
> That's GrowthOS. Drop a comment for early access. Talk soon.

**Total:** ~239 words · ~90 seconds at Marcel's typical pace

## How this script handles the four open questions from the spine

1. **"Closed-loop system" definition** — Beat 3 inlines the definition: *"The frontier AI work — context, research, planning, writing, optimization — all happens inside the platform, end to end."* That's the one-line version. The visual proof is the looping diagram in the B-roll plan.

2. **AEO acronym** — Cut entirely. Replaced with the picture: *"Agents are reading the web on their behalf, building shortlists before anyone ever lands on your site."* Cleaner. No marketer needs an acronym to feel that.

3. **CTA / closing beat** — Soft offer ("drop a comment for early access") in the primary draft. Two alternates above for if Marcel/Harmony want to land on stakes or go with the generosity move.

4. **Receipts for Beat 1** — Script assumes real screenshots on screen (job postings, stack diagrams, tweets). If we'd rather paraphrase, the line can become *"Half of marketing Twitter is telling you to build agents"* — but the receipts version hits harder. Open decision.

---

## What this draft consciously DOES

- **Names the flip in plain English.** "Marketers should become engineers" → "We did the engineering for you." That inversion is the entire point of the launch and the script makes it the hook.
- **Uses Marcel's "receipts" pattern.** Real customer names, not abstract claims.
- **Uses his "unless you've been living under a rock" stakes opener.** It's his signature for "obviously true big shift" framing — appears at least 4× in the CheckThat VSL transcript alone.
- **Ends every beat with a short, landable line.** "I think that's wrong." / "They need leverage, not a side career in engineering." / "That's the whole point." / "Disappear." Each one is a quote-card.

## What this draft consciously DOES NOT

- No demo. No screenshots of the product UI in narration. Visuals only.
- No mention of features, pricing, tiers.
- No "future of marketing" think-piece tone.
- No "AEO" acronym in the spoken script.
- No corporate softeners ("we believe", "we feel", "our mission is to").
