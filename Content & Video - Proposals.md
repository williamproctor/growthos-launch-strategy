## 1. AI-Led Growth Workshop

> **Recommendation: Live workshop with recording, June 12 (10 days post-launch). Gated registration with ICP-matching questions mirroring the survey. Cap at 50 attendees.**

### Why this shape

- The survey's three loudest AEO barriers are "no tool" (25%), "don't know where to start" (22%), and "budget" (21%). A workshop solves #2 directly, weakens #3 (free, but valuable), and de-risks #1 (you don't need a tool to start — you need a system).
- Clean-set report opt-in rate is 86% and advisor opt-in is 60%. Engagement appetite is there; we have 79 clean report opt-ins + 55 advisor opt-ins to seed the invite list.
- Marcel has the authority to teach this — 15 years across IBM, HashiCorp, Scale AI, Deepgram, ServiceTitan ([00:00:00] in the transcript). The workshop is the most concentrated way to *spend* that authority during launch month.
- Live > recorded for the first one. Q&A captures intent signals we can route to sales — same way we used the advisor opt-in field.

### Format

- **Title (proposed):** *"How to Build a Closed-Loop Content System (Without Becoming a Marketing Engineer)"*
- **60 minutes** — 45 min content + 15 min Q&A
- **Zoom Webinar**, registration-required
- **Speaker:** Marcel solo for first one. (Harmony as co-host for second — keep the structural option to add a customer voice later.)
- **Structure:** mirrors the four-beat narrative spine, but operationalized into steps the audience can take home
- **Recording:** posted as gated long-form 7 days later → lead magnet for the nurture sequence
- **Workshop attendees get a 1:1 advisor session offer** (the 60% opt-in rate suggests ~30 of the 50 attendees will take it)

### Audience / registration

- **Open registration** with 3 qualifying questions (role, team size, AEO maturity) — same schema as the GTM-AI survey so we can append answers to the same Supabase table
- **Cap at 50** to keep Q&A intimate. If demand exceeds, run a second session the following week, don't expand the cap.
- **Promotion targets:**
  - 79 clean report opt-ins (warmest)
  - 55 clean advisor opt-ins (warmest+)
  - LinkedIn organic from Marcel + Harmony
  - 39 known LinkedIn-UTM respondents (sequence pre-registered)

### Open questions

- **Date:** June 12 vs. June 19? June 12 lets us announce in launch week and ride momentum. June 19 gives us more runway to fill the seats.
- **Pricing:** free vs. token $25? A small fee filters tire-kickers and raises perceived value, but creates payment friction and contradicts the "we're giving away the playbook" positioning. **My lean: free.**
- **Add a customer voice?** If we have one high-intent lead who'd do a 5-minute "here's what we did" story, that's the strongest social proof we could ship. Worth the ask.

## 3. OS Demos

> **Recommendation: Two tracks. "Cold Demos" (45-second animated workflow vignettes for top-of-funnel) and "Hot Demos" (3-5 minute real-screen captures for mid-funnel + sales).**

### Why two tracks

The survey gave us three distinct objections, and one demo format can't hit all three:

| Objection | % of clean set | Demo type that addresses it |
|---|---|---|
| "Don't have a tool to track it" (no tool) | 25% | Cold demo — show the tool exists, in motion, in 30 seconds |
| "Don't know where to start" | 22% | Hot demo — walk through a real workflow, narrated by Marcel |
| "Budget" | 21% | Both — cold creates demand, hot proves the time saved is worth the spend |

Plus the awareness baseline: zero GrowthOS mentions in the survey. Cold demos are the awareness lever. Hot demos are the conviction lever.

### Workflows to demo — priority order

| # | Workflow | Maps to which survey pain | Maps to which beat |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Auto-publish a researched, brand-voiced page in 30 min** | 52% brand-voice pain + 49% strategic-thinking decline | Beat 3 (closed loop in action) |
| **2** | **AEO signal: see which agents cite your pages and why** | 25% no-tool barrier + 13% "know we should, haven't" | Beat 4 (new stakes, concrete) |
| **3** | **Decay detection: which of your 100 pages need refresh** | 58% attribution pain | Beat 3 (closed loop = system that learns) |
| **4** | **Brand-context ingestion: one truth source for all agents** | 49% "AI creates inconsistency" + Marcel's ICP/positioning quote [00:13:00] | Beat 3 + Beat 2 (relief — "set context once") |
| **5** | **Human-in-the-loop review and learning loop** | Marcel's Waymo analogy made concrete — corrections improve next runs | Beat 3 (the learning part) |

### For Katia (per the checklist sub-bullet)

- **Capture priority:** get Marcel or product to record raw screen captures of Workflows #1-#3 by EOD Friday so Katia can begin abstract-animation work Monday. Animation has the longest lead time of any asset on this list.
- **Format per cold demo:** **both** 9:16 vertical AND 16:9 horizontal exports. Vertical for Instagram/TikTok/LinkedIn-mobile, horizontal for YouTube Shorts/embedded on the marketing site.
- **Captions baked in, no voiceover** for cold demos. VPs of Marketing watch on mute in meetings. Forcing text + motion only also drives scroll-stop on social where audio defaults off.
- **Hot demos:** Marcel's actual VO (real-time, founder voice). That authenticity is the differentiator. Don't over-produce.
- **Music:** library track for cold demos; no music for hot demos.

### Animation style recommendation

Minimal / abstract, in the spirit of **Linear** or **Stripe** product motion — clean geometric primitives, ample whitespace, single color accent. Reasoning:

- Matches the closed-loop narrative (visual: nodes connecting, signals flowing in/out, loop completing)
- Reads as "modern category-defining product" not "AI gadget toy"
- Reuses well across all 4 cold demos with consistent visual grammar
- Doesn't compete with the upcoming Charlie content for "fun" — keeps the product surface serious

### Open questions

- Should we lock the 5 workflows above as-is, or run a 30-min Marcel + product sign-off before Katia starts?
- Are we comfortable showing competitor names (AirOps, NotebookLM) in cold demos to anchor positioning, or do we stay general?
- Cold demo durations: hard cap at 30s or allow up to 60s for Workflow #1?

### First action

Marcel + Katia + Will 30-min sync today to confirm the 5 workflows and start raw screen capture for Workflows #1-3.

## 5. Other long-form content — guides, reports, flagship pieces

> **Recommendation: Ship ONE flagship report + ONE practical guide. Don't dilute by trying to ship more.**

### Flagship: *"The State of AEO in B2B Marketing 2026"* — the survey report

#### Why

- **We already have the data.** 181 raw responses, 92 clean. Even after dropping spam, n=92 is reportable.
- **The most defensible owned research in our category.** No one else has run this study. AirOps doesn't have it. NotebookLM doesn't have it.
- **Launch week press hook:** *"92 verified marketing leaders told us..."* is a much stronger angle than *"we built a thing."* (Note: we're using the audited number — "92 verified" not "181" — because the audit becomes part of the credibility story, see below.)
- **144 raw / 79 clean report opt-ins are waiting** to receive it.
- **Competitive frame:** when AirOps or any AEO-tools vendor tries to claim category leadership, we have data. They have a dashboard.

#### Spec

- **16-20 pages, designed** (not a Google Doc PDF export)
- **Anchor stats from the clean set:** 77% fragmentation, 71% attribution pain, 57% planning AEO investment, 65% Marketing-Engineer disenchantment
- **6-8 voice-of-customer quotes** already curated in the Survey Insights page
- **"Recommended actions for marketers" section** — this is where we differentiate from generic "state of" report templates. Don't just diagnose; prescribe.
- **Gate behind email**; auto-add to the launch nurture sequence
- **Methodology / audit disclosure:** explicitly explain the validation pass. **The 26% spam finding becomes a credibility marker, not a weakness.** Suggested framing: *"Most industry surveys report inflated numbers because they don't audit for response authenticity. Here's what we found when we did, and what we dropped."* This is a category-shaping move — and a quiet shot at the typical "we surveyed 500 marketers!" reports that don't disclose methodology.

#### Ship

- **Launch week.** Day 1 email to 79 clean report opt-ins. Gated download on marketing site by Day 2.

#### Owner

- I can produce the writing draft + page-design brief by EOW. Needs designer + Harmony review.

### Practical guide: *"The AEO Playbook: A 4-Step System Without 12 Tools"*

#### Why

- Survey's #1 barrier among AEO-planning respondents: "no tool to track it" (29%)
- #2 barrier: "budget" (28%)
- A free playbook addresses both — gives them a system without spending a dollar — and naturally ladders into "and here's the tool that does steps 2-4 for you" = GrowthOS pitch without the brochure feel
- Recommended in the Survey Insights doc yesterday; this confirms the spec

#### Spec

- **8-10 pages, practical, screenshot-heavy**
- **Four steps map to the four beats** of the launch narrative
- Each step ends with a *"What GrowthOS automates here"* sidebar — never the main thrust
- Less designed than the State of AEO report. Functional, not brochure. Implies *"use this, don't just admire it."*

#### Ship

- **2 weeks post-launch** (Jun 16). The State of AEO report does the awareness lift first; the playbook converts the audience that lands from it.

- ❌ **A multi-author "industry roundtable" piece** — dilutes Marcel as category-definer
- ❌ **A 50-page ContentOS user manual / docs PDF** — should live in the product, not in marketing
- ❌ **An "Ultimate Guide to AI Marketing"** — generic, undifferentiated, signals desperation for SEO traffic

- **Distribution beyond owned channels:** do we pitch the State of AEO report to TechCrunch / Marketing Brew / Lenny's Newsletter, or stay owned-only? My lean: pitch Marketing Brew specifically.
