## 1. AI-Led Growth Workshop

> **Recommendation: Live workshop with recording. Position it as a *platform argument*, not a how-to — we are NOT teaching the closed-loop IP. Include a demo of what a fully-loaded content pipeline COULD look like in GrowthOS. Cap at 50 attendees.**

### The reframe (important)

The first draft of this proposal had the workshop titled *"How to Build a Closed-Loop Content System."* That gives away the IP and trains attendees to compete with us. The corrected framing:

- **Diagnose** the trap publicly — the "Marketing Engineer / GTM Engineer / Growth Engineer" pressure, dashboard fatigue, blank-canvas builder sprawl
- **Argue** that a platform is the right shape of solution for the moment — conceptual, not implementation-specific
- **Show**, via a demo, what a scaled content pipeline could look like running on GrowthOS — output, not internals

That's the workshop. We never teach how the loop closes; we argue why a platform that closes it is the answer, and we let the product speak for itself in the demo.

### Why this shape

- Survey's three loudest AEO barriers are **"no tool" (25%)**, **"don't know where to start" (22%)**, and **"budget" (21%)**. The workshop addresses #2 by reframing the problem (you don't need to figure out a build, you need a platform), and weakens #1 and #3 by letting attendees *see* what a fully-loaded pipeline does.
- **65%** of the clean set said they're losing core skills to AI tool sprawl. The "Marketing Engineer" framing currently saturating LinkedIn is the noise we're naming directly — the workshop is the most concentrated forum to do that.
- Marcel's own language [00:18:34] already gives us the argument: AirOps-style stitching and AEO-probe dashboards are *"borderline intellectually dishonest."* The workshop says that publicly, with proof, without revealing the inside of our system.
- Clean-set report opt-in is 86% and advisor opt-in is 60%. We have **79 clean report opt-ins + 55 advisor opt-ins** to seed the invite list.
- Live > recorded for the first one. Q&A captures intent signals we route to sales.

### Format

- **Title (proposed):** *"The Marketing Engineer Trap: Why You Can't Build Your Way Out of AEO Chaos"*
  - Alt: *"Why Marketers Don't Need to Become Engineers — A Platform Argument"*
- **60 minutes** — 35 min argument + 15 min demo + 10 min Q&A
- **Zoom Webinar**, registration-required
- **Speaker:** Marcel solo. Keep the structural option to add a customer voice in later sessions.
- Recording posted as gated long-form afterward → lead magnet for nurture
- Workshop attendees get a 1:1 advisor session offer (60% historic opt-in rate suggests ~30 of the 50 will take it)

### Content arc (the platform argument, not the how-to)

| Min | Section | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **0-10** | **Diagnose the trap** | Survey data on the table. Marketing Engineer / GTM Engineer / Growth Engineer pressure. Dashboard fatigue. *"This isn't your fault — here's why the market is fragmenting."* |
| **10-20** | **Why building doesn't scale** | Blank-canvas tools (AirOps-style stitching, Zapier sprawl, in-house glue) require engineers you don't have AND produce one-off pipelines that don't learn. Named with examples. |
| **20-35** | **The platform argument** | *Why* a single platform with closed-loop properties is the right shape of solution. Conceptual only. No teaching of HOW to build it. |
| **35-50** | **Demo: what a scaled pipeline could look like in GrowthOS** | Marcel walks through 2 workflows from the OS Demos prioritization — recommended: Workflow #1 (auto-publish brand-voiced page) + Workflow #3 (decay detection at scale). Shows OUTPUT, not internals. |
| **50-60** | **Q&A** | Captures intent, surfaces objections that feed sales. |

### Audience / registration

- Open registration with 3 qualifying questions (role, team size, AEO maturity) — same schema as the GTM-AI survey so we can append to the same Supabase table
- **Cap at 50** to keep Q&A intimate. If demand exceeds, run a second session, don't expand the cap.
- **Promotion targets:** 79 clean report opt-ins (warmest), 55 advisor opt-ins (warmest+), LinkedIn organic from Marcel + Harmony, 39 known-LinkedIn-UTM respondents

### Open questions

- **Pricing:** free vs. token $25? Lean: **free** — fits the "we're naming the trap and showing you out of it" positioning.
- **Customer voice?** If one of the 12 verified high-intent leads would share a 5-minute "here's our content pain" story, it strengthens the diagnose phase without us claiming wins we haven't earned yet.
- **Recording release:** post the recording as gated long-form, or keep the demo portion behind a stricter wall (since it's product footage)?
- **Demo depth:** show real GrowthOS UI, or a polished narrated walkthrough? Real UI is more honest; narrated is safer if anything in product is mid-build.
- **What we explicitly DO NOT cover:** the implementation of the loop, the model orchestration, the brand-voice ingestion mechanism, the citation-detection signal pipeline. These are the IP — they appear as *capabilities visible in the demo*, never as *how-to instructions*.

Lock the framing and the two demo workflows with Marcel. Registration page can ship once topic + speaker are confirmed.

## 2. Podcast / Long-Form Video

> **Recommendation: YES. Ship as "Whiteboard Sessions with Marcel" — biweekly cadence, video-first, surrogate format. George as surrogate (not Harmony).**

### Why YES

- The survey's voice-of-customer field is begging for this format. Real respondent quotes from yesterday:
  - *"I want AI agents to do tasks 'end-to-end' in an autonomous way and ping me as a leader with updates."*
  - *"I wish AI could reliably act as an execution-ready GTM operator, not just an assistant."*
  - These are people asking for the GrowthOS thesis without knowing GrowthOS exists. Long-form video is the format that closes that gap (a tweet can't).
- Competitive landscape: NotebookLM (11 mentions), AirOps (4), Granola (8) are all currently "explained" via marketing copy and feature pages. **None of them have a Marcel — a category-defining founder who can teach the thesis on a whiteboard.** That's our wedge.
- Marcel's transcript proves he can talk for 30+ minutes coherently in his own voice. The raw material is already there.

### Sub-question answers (from the checklist)

**"Conversation with a surrogate new customer"** — yes, this is the format.

- **George recommended over Harmony.** Reasoning from yesterday's surrogate card: George works with GrowthX customers directly and can carry the "I'm a customer who'd be using this" energy without breaking the production-vs-on-camera duality (Will is producing; Harmony is needed for CMO-level messaging arbitration).

- **Surrogate's questions** come straight from the survey's open text fields. *"I don't know where to start. I'm a marketer, not an engineer. Why is your tool different from the dashboards I've seen?"*

**"How much product detail / how to extract product info from Marcel / whiteboard content?"**

- **70/30 split per episode:** 70% on the principle and the mental model, 30% on how GrowthOS instantiates it.
- **Whiteboard YES — explicitly.** Marcel's Waymo analogy ([00:22:52] in the Quotes Bank) is the most begging-to-be-drawn analogy in the entire transcript. We literally lose value by not putting it on a whiteboard.
- **Use the Quotes Bank arc per episode:** open with a Beat 1 quote (the recognition moment), middle on Beat 3 (the bet, with Marcel drawing the closed loop), close on Beat 4 (stakes). Same architecture as the launch video, but unpacked.

**"How in the weeds on core product differentiators?"**

- **Conceptual, not feature-level, for Episodes 1-3.** Save feature deep-dives for OS Demos (#3 below) and the product page.
- Differentiation language is already locked in Marcel's own words: closed-loop vs. dashboards ([00:18:34] — *"borderline intellectually dishonest"*), closed-loop vs. blank-canvas builders ([00:18:42] — *"200 nodes, go stitch those nodes together"*), closed-loop vs. AEO-probe tools ([00:17:45]).
- **Risk to avoid:** turning the podcast into a 30-minute feature reel. That's what kills founder-led shows. Marcel teaches a worldview; the worldview makes the product obvious.

Reason: VPs of Marketing — our primary ICP per the survey — consume long-form podcast content with depth, not frequency. Weekly cadence creates production load that compromises quality, burns Marcel's calendar, and lowers per-episode shareability. Biweekly lets us land each one.

## 3. OS Demos

> **Recommendation: Two tracks. "Cold Demos" (45-second animated workflow vignettes for top-of-funnel) and "Hot Demos" (3-5 minute real-screen captures for mid-funnel + sales).**

### Why two tracks

The survey gave us three distinct objections, and one demo format can't hit all three:

| Objection | % of clean set | Demo type that addresses it |
|---|---|---|
| "Don't have a tool to track it" (no tool) | 25% | Cold demo — show the tool exists, in motion, in 30 seconds |
| "Don't know where to start" | 22% | Hot demo — walk through a real workflow, narrated by Marcel |
| "Budget" | 21% | Both — cold creates demand, hot proves the time saved is worth the spend |

Plus the awareness baseline: zero GrowthOS mentions in the survey. Cold demos are the awareness lever. Hot demos are the conviction lever.

### Workflows to demo — priority order

| # | Workflow | Maps to which survey pain | Maps to which beat |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Auto-publish a researched, brand-voiced page in 30 min** | 52% brand-voice pain + 49% strategic-thinking decline | Beat 3 (closed loop in action) |
| **2** | **AEO signal: see which agents cite your pages and why** | 25% no-tool barrier + 13% "know we should, haven't" | Beat 4 (new stakes, concrete) |
| **3** | **Decay detection: which of your 100 pages need refresh** | 58% attribution pain | Beat 3 (closed loop = system that learns) |
| **4** | **Brand-context ingestion: one truth source for all agents** | 49% "AI creates inconsistency" + Marcel's ICP/positioning quote [00:13:00] | Beat 3 + Beat 2 (relief — "set context once") |
| **5** | **Human-in-the-loop review and learning loop** | Marcel's Waymo analogy made concrete — corrections improve next runs | Beat 3 (the learning part) |

### For Katia (per the checklist sub-bullet)

- **Capture priority:** raw screen captures of Workflows #1-#3 are the first thing to record once the workflows are locked. Animation has the longest lead time of any asset on this list, so Katia is unblocked the moment captures land.
- **Format per cold demo:** **both** 9:16 vertical AND 16:9 horizontal exports. Vertical for Instagram/TikTok/LinkedIn-mobile, horizontal for YouTube Shorts/embedded on the marketing site.
- **Captions baked in, no voiceover** for cold demos. VPs of Marketing watch on mute in meetings. Forcing text + motion only also drives scroll-stop on social where audio defaults off.
- **Hot demos:** Marcel's actual VO (real-time, founder voice). That authenticity is the differentiator. Don't over-produce.
- **Music:** library track for cold demos; no music for hot demos.

### Animation style recommendation

Minimal / abstract, in the spirit of **Linear** or **Stripe** product motion — clean geometric primitives, ample whitespace, single color accent. Reasoning:

- Matches the closed-loop narrative (visual: nodes connecting, signals flowing in/out, loop completing)
- Reads as "modern category-defining product" not "AI gadget toy"
- Reuses well across all 4 cold demos with consistent visual grammar
- Doesn't compete with the upcoming Charlie content for "fun" — keeps the product surface serious

### Open questions

- Should we lock the 5 workflows above as-is, or run a 30-min Marcel + product sign-off before Katia starts?
- Are we comfortable showing competitor names (AirOps, NotebookLM) in cold demos to anchor positioning, or do we stay general?
- Cold demo durations: hard cap at 30s or allow up to 60s for Workflow #1?

### First action

Marcel + Katia + Will 30-min sync to confirm the 5 workflows and start raw screen capture for Workflows #1-3.

## 5. Other long-form content — guides, reports, flagship pieces

> **Recommendation: Ship ONE flagship report + ONE practical guide. Don't dilute by trying to ship more.**

### Flagship: *"The State of AEO in B2B Marketing 2026"* — the survey report

#### Why

- **We already have the data.** 181 raw responses, 92 clean. Even after dropping spam, n=92 is reportable.
- **The most defensible owned research in our category.** No one else has run this study. AirOps doesn't have it. NotebookLM doesn't have it.
- **Launch week press hook:** *"92 verified marketing leaders told us..."* is a much stronger angle than *"we built a thing."* (Note: we're using the audited number — "92 verified" not "181" — because the audit becomes part of the credibility story, see below.)
- **144 raw / 79 clean report opt-ins are waiting** to receive it.
- **Competitive frame:** when AirOps or any AEO-tools vendor tries to claim category leadership, we have data. They have a dashboard.

#### Spec

- **16-20 pages, designed** (not a Google Doc PDF export)
- **Anchor stats from the clean set:** 77% fragmentation, 71% attribution pain, 57% planning AEO investment, 65% Marketing-Engineer disenchantment
- **6-8 voice-of-customer quotes** already curated in the Survey Insights page
- **"Recommended actions for marketers" section** — this is where we differentiate from generic "state of" report templates. Don't just diagnose; prescribe.
- **Gate behind email**; auto-add to the launch nurture sequence
- **Methodology / audit disclosure:** explicitly explain the validation pass. **The 26% spam finding becomes a credibility marker, not a weakness.** Suggested framing: *"Most industry surveys report inflated numbers because they don't audit for response authenticity. Here's what we found when we did, and what we dropped."* This is a category-shaping move — and a quiet shot at the typical "we surveyed 500 marketers!" reports that don't disclose methodology.

#### Ship

- **Launch week.** Day 1 email to 79 clean report opt-ins. Gated download on the marketing site available at launch.

#### Owner

- Will produces the writing draft + page-design brief. Needs designer + Harmony review.

### Practical guide: *"The AEO Playbook: A 4-Step System Without 12 Tools"*

#### Why

- Survey's #1 barrier among AEO-planning respondents: "no tool to track it" (29%)
- #2 barrier: "budget" (28%)
- A free playbook addresses both — gives them a system without spending a dollar — and naturally ladders into "and here's the tool that does steps 2-4 for you" = GrowthOS pitch without the brochure feel
- Recommended in the Survey Insights doc yesterday; this confirms the spec

#### Spec

- **8-10 pages, practical, screenshot-heavy**
- **Four steps map to the four beats** of the launch narrative
- Each step ends with a *"What GrowthOS automates here"* sidebar — never the main thrust
- Less designed than the State of AEO report. Functional, not brochure. Implies *"use this, don't just admire it."*

#### Ship

- **Follow-on, after the report has done the awareness lift.** The State of AEO report runs first; the playbook converts the audience that lands from it.

### What NOT to ship (deliberate cuts)

I'd actively recommend AGAINST these even if they come up in the meeting:

- ❌ **A "future of marketing" essay or manifesto** — already covered by the launch video itself; would dilute Marcel's positioning shot
- ❌ **A multi-author "industry roundtable" piece** — dilutes Marcel as category-definer
- ❌ **A 50-page ContentOS user manual / docs PDF** — should live in the product, not in marketing
- ❌ **An "Ultimate Guide to AI Marketing"** — generic, undifferentiated, signals desperation for SEO traffic

### Open questions

- **Branding:** *"GrowthX presents…"*, *"GrowthOS presents…"*, or Marcel-bylined personal piece?
- **Co-authorship:** should Harmony or George get bylines for credibility / network leverage?
- **Distribution beyond owned channels:** do we pitch the State of AEO report to TechCrunch / Marketing Brew / Lenny's Newsletter, or stay owned-only? My lean: pitch Marketing Brew specifically.

Confirm the State of AEO report scope so a writing brief can land.
