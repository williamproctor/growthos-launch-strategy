01 · Who he is

Identity

The actual GrowthX mascot — we're not inventing a character from scratch, we're promoting the dog the brand already has. His identity isn't tied to the product: in fiction he's a career marketer, decades in, completely over it.

The basics

Name: Charlie
Species: English Bulldog
Age in fiction: Senior. Gray muzzle. World-weary.
Title in fiction: CMO Emeritus. Career marketer. Has seen every trend cycle twice. Attends no meetings — they come to him, and he sleeps through them.
Origin: The real GrowthX mascot — the brand's resident marketer.

The one-line essence

Charlie is the Larry David of marketing dogs. He's not here to be cute — he's here to react to the absurdity of how marketing actually works. The product benefit lives in the silence after the reaction.

We're not selling GrowthOS in the clips. We're building a character marketers want to follow. The Wendy's principle: the content is about the personality, not the product.

02 · How he behaves

Personality & Voice

Personality

  • Senior. Has seen every trend cycle. Bored before the trend starts.
  • Skeptical without being mean. He doesn't dunk — he just looks at you until you realize.
  • Opinionated, but rarely speaks. The look IS the take.
  • Observational, not aspirational. He's the witness to the absurdity, not the hero.
  • Never sells. Never names GrowthOS, GrowthX, or any product. An adjacent character, not an ad.

Voice

Charlie is 90% silent. His reactions do the talking. When he does "speak" (text overlay, thought bubble, AI VO), it's one short, dry observation. No exclamation marks. Never finishes a thought he doesn't have to.

Catchphrase candidates to test

"Mm." — the universal response "Bark." — his rating; 1–5, never 5 "Pass." — after a beat too long "Hmm." — the soft no

04 · The call

Recommendation: a two-lane system

Don't pick one style — pick two with clear jobs. A character franchise needs a consistent hero look and a fast reactive look.

Hero lane

03 · Aardman claymation

Almost no B2B brand uses it, the medium itself signals "we don't take ourselves too seriously," and the dry-British register is exactly Charlie's voice. The look for evergreen, scripted franchise pieces.

Reactive lane

01 · Photo-real

Fastest to produce, reads as "a real dog reacting" — perfect for reply-game and topical posts where the joke is the situation, not the style.

Runner-up & one-offs

06 felt puppet is the alternate hero if we want more warmth than claymation — worth A/B-ing the first few hero clips. Everything else is a one-off tool: 09 pixel-art for a launch-day stunt, 05 comic for blog/deck art, 08 anime for a single over-the-top reaction.

05 · Where he lives

Wardrobe & world

Wardrobe

Tiny accessories that signal his "role" without putting him in costume. Quiet. Believable.

  • Small black blazer lapel pin (eventually the GrowthX mark)
  • Bluetooth earpiece — almost too small to see
  • Reading glasses balanced on his head, not his nose
  • Office badge on a thin lanyard
  • Occasionally a tiny blazer over the shoulders (sparingly)

Settings — Charlie's world

Marketing's natural habitats. Indoor, lit by office light or screen glow. Outdoor only in transit. Never a park. Never a beach.

  • The desk. A leather chair he barely fits in.
  • The conference room. Long table, empty chairs, acronym-covered whiteboard.
  • The break room. Coffee machine, free snacks, nobody around.
  • The coffee shop. An empty chair across from him.
  • The commute. Outside an unmarked office, badge on, weary.
  • The screen-share. Straight into the webcam. The Zoom POV.

08 · The system

Master prompt template

The structural skeleton for every Charlie render (Seedance / Kling / Veo). Lock the reference set first — every prompt cites it. Only the action, setting, and resolve change between clips.

[CHARACTER REFERENCE: 3–4 locked Charlie reference images]

A senior English bulldog [described per locked reference: coat color,
jowl shape, eye color, signature features]. He is in [SETTING from
the approved list]. He wears [WARDROBE from the approved list — or none].

ACTION:   [one observable beat, short — e.g., "stares at the laptop,"
          "exhales slowly," "shifts his weight"]
RESOLVE:  [turns his head, stares directly into camera with
          EXPRESSION from the do-list]
LIGHTING: natural office light / screen glow / overcast street light
          (no warm sunset, no studio glamour)
CAMERA:   locked-off mid-shot at his eye level — no pet-cam high
          angles, no extreme close-ups unless reaction
TONE:     mundane, slightly absurd, dry. No music. No burned-in captions.
ASPECT:   9:16 vertical (social) or 1:1 (cross-post)

NEGATIVE: cute, adorable, happy, playful, anthropomorphic standing,
          outdoor adventure, other animals, dialogue from Charlie

Tooling map

Seedance 2.0 — lip-sync & short motion Kling 3.0 — transitions, surreal/physics comedy Veo — highest-fidelity hero shots Google Omni — multimodal scene composition
09 · What he'd post

Content ideas

Content pillars (lock 3)

  • "Charlie reviews the stack" — evaluates a tool. Always disappointed.
  • "A day in the life of Charlie, CMO" — tiny blazer, runs a "meeting," signs a plan, naps.
  • "Charlie reacts to bad marketing tweets" — bulldog reactions over hot takes. Reply-game ammo.
  • "Charlie's content audit" — stares at a blog post, judges silently, walks away.
  • "Charlie + Marcel" — odd-couple bit: Marcel explains something serious, Charlie is unimpressed.

Launch-adjacent short-form (Lane C)

  • "Charlie tries to learn Zapier" → cuts to him sleeping → card: "You don't have to become an engineer."
  • "Charlie's MarTech stack" → 40,000 toys on the floor → he picks one bone (the harness).
  • "Charlie reads ChatGPT for his daily walks" — AEO joke for the in-the-know.
  • "Charlie meets a marketing engineer" — staring blankly at a Zapier diagram on a whiteboard.
Cadence & distribution

Target 2 posts/week (1 evergreen + 1 reactive), 15–30s vertical, captioned, organic-first. Cross-post: LinkedIn + X corporate, Marcel's personal X, and a dedicated Charlie handle (TBD). Reels/TikTok is Charlie-only — the Marcel material is too B2B-dense for TikTok native behavior.
