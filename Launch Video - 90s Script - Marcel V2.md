## What V1 got wrong (and why it sounded AI-written)

I want to be honest about the diagnosis, because the pattern matters more than this one script.

1. **Bloodless opener.** V1 opened *"Hey, what's up everyone? Marcel here. Ready for a hot take?"* — three Marcel-recognizable phrases stacked into one breath. That's a pastiche, not a voice. Real Marcel uses ONE entry move and then gets into the scene.
2. **Pure assertion in Beat 1, no scene.** V1 described the consensus from 30,000 feet. The viewer had nothing to grab. Every effective modern launch video opens with a *specific moment* — Stripe shows a merchant struggling with payments, Loom shows a calendar full of meetings, Cluely shows a guy lying on a date. V1 had no such scene. V2 opens with a CMO, a Notion screen, a specific dollar number, and a laugh.
3. **List-based receipts instead of a story.** V1 dropped five customer names (Lovable, Webflow, Ramp, Brex, Sentinel One) in a single sentence. Marcel does that pattern in 25-minute talks where he has room. In 90 seconds it's filler. V2 drops the list entirely and uses ONE specific person (the CMO with 12 tools).
4. **Used "leverage" as a verb.** I explicitly flagged in my V1 voice analysis that Marcel doesn't use that word, then put it in the script. Caught.
5. **The inversion got buried.** "We do the engineering for you" is the entire thesis of the launch. V1 made it the third sentence of Beat 3. V2 makes it the headline of the flip: *"Marketers shouldn't have to become engineers. They should have a system that already DID the engineering."*
6. **Stakes got over-orchestrated.** V1 escalated three times in Beat 4 (biggest shift → agents reading → scale or disappear). Real Marcel says a thing and tells you why it matters once. V2 has one picture (the agent building shortlists), one rhetorical move (your homepage isn't your homepage), one stakes line.
7. **AI transition phrases.** V1 had "And here's why this matters right now" and "That's why we built ContentOS." These are AI stitching phrases. Marcel just starts new thoughts. V2 cuts them entirely — the junction cuts in the video do the transition work.
8. **Template-y CTA.** "Drop a comment for early access" could be any SaaS launch. V2 borrows Marcel's actual CheckThat CTA structure: personal offer ("I'll DM you personally"), low-friction trigger ("comment 'OS'"), warm close ("Talk soon").
9. **No texture.** V1 was all argument, no human edges. V2 has *"She just laughed."* That single line — a specific tactile detail — does more work than any of V1's claims.

## What modern founder-led launch videos do (from the research pass)

- **Don't open with a logo or "introducing X."** The 0–3 second hook should be the problem your buyer wakes up thinking about, or a specific scene that makes them feel seen. Sources from the SaaS launch video literature agree: starting with a relatable frustration converts ~22% better than starting with a brand intro.
- **One mental shift, not multiple.** Pick the single "wow" / pivot moment and put every beat in service of landing it.
- **AIDA still works for B2B SaaS launches in 2026.** Attention (0–8s, the hook scene) → Interest (8–30s, agitate by naming the broken consensus) → Desire (30–60s, position your product as the obvious answer with a transformed-state demonstration) → Action (final 5s, one frictionless CTA).
- **Founder-voiced = trust play.** Direct-to-camera with the founder builds the trust signal AI-generated competitors can't match. Don't hide behind animation, narrator VO, or an actor.
- **Be opinionated.** The Cluely template (love it or hate it) and the Stripe template (radically understated) both work because they have a strong point of view. The unifying property is opinion, not loudness.
- **End with a specific, low-friction action.** Not "contact us" — something like *"Comment X and I'll DM you"* or *"Start your free trial."* The CTA is the conversion event, not a soft outro.
- **For B2B founder videos: lead with story, end with stakes.** Stripe/Loom/Linear all follow this. Open with a real person in pain → name the pattern → reveal the bet → name the stakes → offer.

## V2 — Annotated with delivery notes

### OPENER · SCENE — *the hook*

> "Hey, what's up everyone, Marcel here."

→ Marcel's signature open, but JUST THIS. No "ready for a hot take." The hook is the next line, not the greeting.

> "I had a call yesterday with a CMO. She pulls up her Notion and shows me her 'AI stack.' Twelve tools. Eleven grand a month. I asked her: 'Has any of this moved your pipeline?'"

→ Tense shift to present ("she pulls up") is a deliberate storytelling move. Marcel does this in the CheckThat VSL. It puts the viewer *in the room.*
→ Specific numbers ("twelve," "eleven grand") do the heavy lifting. NEVER round these up — specific = real.
→ **MOMENT FLAGGED FOR MARCEL:** If you have a fresher real anecdote from this week, swap it in. The structural job of this beat is: place the viewer in a specific scene with a specific person and a specific number, ending on a small human moment ("she laughed"). The "yesterday" framing is a present-tense crutch — adapt to your truth.

> "She just laughed."

→ Land it. ~1 second of silence after. This is the line that gets clipped for the cold open of the social cuts. Don't rush past it.

### CONSENSUS — *name what they're hearing*

> "This is what they're telling marketers right now: to keep your job, you need to become a 'Marketing Engineer.' Learn the APIs. Build the agents. Wire up the stack."

→ Air quotes on *Marketing Engineer* — visible, not just vocal.
→ Three-beat list. Stay clipped. Don't elaborate on any one.
→ "Wire up the stack" — this phrase comes back in the next beat as a callback. Plant it cleanly.

> "I think it's the dumbest pitch in B2B right now."

→ Strongest opinion in the script. Marcel-direct. Don't soften it ("kind of") or qualify ("for some companies"). Either say it or don't.
→ Half-second beat, then cut to flip.

### THE FLIP / REVEAL — *the headline*

> "Marketers shouldn't have to become engineers. They should have a system that already DID the engineering."

→ This is THE LINE. Every other line in the script exists to set this up and pay it off. Slow. Let it breathe. Eye contact. This is the screenshot.
→ Emphasis on **DID** — past tense, deliberate. The engineering is *done.*

> "That's what we built. ContentOS."

→ Reveal cleanly. No "introducing," no "we're excited to launch." Just: that's what we built. Name. Move on.

> "We wired the best frontier AI into a closed-loop content system. Research, planning, drafting, optimization — end to end. You bring the strategy. You bring the taste. The system does the rest."

→ **"Wired" callback** to the consensus beat ("wire up the stack"). Subtle but powerful — Marcel just said "we did the wiring so you don't have to" without saying it.
→ The list (research, planning, drafting, optimization) defines "closed-loop" in 5 words. No diagram required for the spoken script, though B-roll can amplify with an animated loop overlay.
→ "Strategy" + "taste" + "the system does the rest" = three-beat rhythm. Lands.
→ "Taste" is the modern AI-era word. Every smart operator is using it. It signals Marcel is current without being trend-y.

### STAKES — *the urgency*

> "Your buyers stopped Googling."

→ Cold-open this beat. No transition. Marcel just starts a new thought. Slight forward lean if shooting close-up.

> "Right now, today, an AI agent is reading the web for them, building shortlists before you even know they're in market."

→ "Right now, today" is a Marcel pattern. Stack two time-markers for emphasis.
→ "Before you even know they're in market" — personalizes the stakes for the marketer (not a generic "buyers behave differently").

> "They might never land on your homepage. And if they do, your homepage isn't your homepage anymore. It's how AI describes you to the room."

→ "Your homepage isn't your homepage anymore" — rhetorical inversion Marcel uses naturally (compare: "It's not a tracking tool. It's a contextual understanding layer.")
→ "To the room" — quiet B2B board-meeting reference. Doesn't need explanation.

> "Brands that publish trustworthy content at scale own this. Everyone else disappears."

→ Clean stakes binary. Don't dilute by adding qualifiers.
→ "Disappears" — final word, lands hard. ~1 second of silence before the offer.

### OFFER

> "I'm letting a small group into ContentOS early. Comment 'OS' below and I'll DM you personally. Talk soon."

→ Marcel's CheckThat-style CTA: scarcity ("small group") + low-friction trigger ("comment OS") + personal touch ("I'll DM you personally") + warm close ("Talk soon").
→ Don't push. Marcel's CTAs are always conversational, never sales-y. Almost throw it away.
